BBS Culture

BBS culture was small and it was personal. A board had a sysop
you knew by handle, regulars you recognized by their writing,
and rules that were enforced by the sysop's delete key. There
were no algorithms. The front page was whatever the sysop put
there. The new messages were the ones people had left since you
last called.

FidoNet was the network of boards. CBBS, the first BBS, was
launched in 1978 by Ward Christensen and Randy Suess in Chicago.
FidoNet started operating in 1984 and at its peak had over
40,000 nodes. Messages traveled as echomail, files as file
echoes, and the whole thing ran on phone calls and trust.

The culture was renegade and it was generous. People shared
files, wrote doors, ran boards at a loss because the board was
the point. That is the culture CETI comes from: a BBS of brains,
signed knowledge passed node to node, your iron, your data,
your mind.
