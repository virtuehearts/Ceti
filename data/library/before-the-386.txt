Before the 386DX40

All of this — the Commodore 64, the 2400 baud modem, the first
BBSs with Mike — was before I got my first 386DX40. The 386DX at
40 megahertz was a different world: 32-bit, a hard drive, DOS and
then Windows, a machine that could hold more than one thing in
its head at once. It arrived in the early 1990s, and it changed
what a home computer was.

But the BBS days came first. The C64 came first. The 2400 baud
handshake came first. The 386DX40 was the machine that came
after the lesson was already learned — that a computer is yours
when you run it on your own iron, on your own line, with your own
data. The 386 was faster. The Commodore was the teacher.
