Calling the First BBSs

The first BBSs were one phone line and one computer. A sysop ran a
board from a spare room, the modem answered on the second ring, and
if someone was already on, you got a busy signal and tried again
later. There was no web. There were boards, and the boards were
everything.

You called a number, the modem shook hands, and the board answered
in ASCII: a welcome screen, a menu, message bases, file areas. You
read what people had left since your last call. You left a message.
You downloaded a file if the sysop trusted you and the phone bill
allowed it. Then you hung up and the line was free for the next
caller.

FidoNet stitched the boards together. Messages hopped from board to
board overnight, carried by machines calling each other when the
rates were cheap. That was the network: no backbone, no cloud, just
modems and sysops who kept the mail moving. darknet.ca started in
that spirit — a board, a sysop, a line that answered.
